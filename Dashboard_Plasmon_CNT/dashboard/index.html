Surface Polariton Dispersion

Application Guide

How to use the Dashboard

This interactive dashboard computes and visualizes the dispersion relations of Surface Polaritons (SPs) in a three-layer asymmetric waveguide structure: Top Dielectric (ε₁) / Metal Film (thickness h) / Bottom Dielectric (ε₃).

  1. Set the Material Parameters: Use the left sidebar to adjust the dielectric constants (ε₁, ε₃) and the metal properties (Plasma frequency ωp, Damping rate Γ). The solver computes your changes automatically as you release the sliders.
  2. Observe the Dispersion: The top-left plot displays the real part of the propagation constant βR against frequency ω. Watch how changing the film thickness h causes the modes to couple and split or merge into the single-interface limits.
  3. Select a Specific Frequency: Use the "Selected f (THz)" slider to sample a specific frequency. A vertical dashed line marks your selection on the main plots.
  4. Analyze the Computed State: The exact numerical roots (both bound β₁/β₂ and leaky βL₁/βL₂) at the selected frequency are printed in the COMPUTED AT ω box, along with derived Physical metrics like the propagation length (Lp) in real meters.
  5. View the Transverse Profile: The bottom-right plot visualizes the field intensity |f(z)|² across the depth of the waveguide structure at your selected frequency.
  6. Interact with Plots: You can interact with the graphs directly: Scroll to Zoom, Drag to Pan, and Double-click to Reset the view.

Pro-tip: If you enter an invalid domain (e.g. searching for bound modes at frequencies above the asymptotic limit), you may see the curves disappear or vanish into the leaky regime. Use the Parameters and Curves buttons for physics guidance!

Computed Values & Legend Explanation

Numerical Roots

β₁, β₂ (Bound Modes): The complex propagation constants of the coupled bound surface polaritons. Real part gives the spatial oscillation frequency. Imaginary part gives the exponential decay (loss).

βL₁, βL₂ (Leaky Modes): The complex propagation constants of the radiative leaky modes. These physically leak energy away from the metal interfaces into the bulk dielectric regions.

Live Sampled State

ε_m: The complex permittivity of the metal at the chosen frequency ω. Calculated from the chosen ωp and Γ.

ε₁, ε₃, ωp, Γ, h: Your inputted system parameters.

ω: The absolute physical frequency currently selected by the Selected f (THz) slider.

k₀ index: The internal array index of the frequency point currently being queried.

Derived Figures of Merit

Lₚ = 1 / (2·Im[β]) (meters): The physical propagation length of each individual mode. Demonstrates how far the wave propagates parallel directly to the interface before its intensity drops by exactly 1/e (≈37%). Notice how tightly confined modes typically have much shorter propagation lengths due to high energy density in the lossy metal.

Legend Glossary
  • ━━ Mode β₁ — SP₁ (ε₁ side): The bound mode more confined to the top ε₁ cladding.
  • ━━ Mode β₂ — SP₃ (ε₃ side): The bound mode more confined to the bottom ε₃ cladding.
  • ━━ Mode βL₁ — leaky (+S₁, −S₃): Leaky wave radiating into bottom cladding.
  • ━━ Mode βL₂ — leaky (−S₁, +S₃): Leaky wave radiating into top cladding.

Input Parameters & Drude Model Reference

ε₁ & ε₃ (Unitless)
The relative permittivity (dielectric constant) of the top (cladding) and bottom (substrate) layers. For bound Surface Polariton modes to exist, we require materials acting as positive dielectrics facing a negative permittivity metal: ε₁ > 0 and ε₃ > 0. The asymmetry ε₁ ≠ ε₃ drives mode cutoff conditions.

ωp (rad/s) — The Plasma Frequency
The natural frequency of collective electron oscillations in the metal. This acts as the fundamental scaling frequency of the system. Frequencies above ωp cause the metal to behave like an ordinary transparent dielectric interface, whereas below ωp, it acts as an opaque metal supporting surface waves.

Γ (rad/s) — The Drude Damping Rate
Governs the collision frequency of electrons in the lattice. A non-zero Γ introduces an imaginary component to the metal's permittivity, representing ohmic/absorptive losses, which in turn causes the propagation constant β to become complex.

h (nm) — Physical Thickness
The physical thickness of the central metal film. As h → ∞, the two interfaces decouple yielding the single-interface polaritons SP₁(∞) and SP₃(∞). As h shrinks, the evanescent tails from each surface overlap, lifting the degeneracy and creating symmetric and antisymmetric coupled modes.

The Drude Model Formula
εm(ω) = ε∞ − ωp² / [ω(ω + iΓ)]

Which expands into imaginary analytical form as:
Re[εm] = const. − (ωp²) / (ω² + Γ²)
Im[εm] = (ωp² · Γ) / [ω(ω² + Γ²)]

Note: For root-finding stability at ultra-low tolerance levels (1e-12), the C++ solver internally normalizes the frequency grid k₀ to ω/ωp and scales spatial variables proportionally to c/ωp.

Curves & Physics Parameters Reference

Dispersion Curves (top-left plot)

━━ β₁ : Bound Long-Range SP — Bound SP mode localized at the ε₁ (high-index) side. As thickness h→∞, converges to the single-interface SP₁(∞) limit. Has symmetric-like transverse field coupling.

━━ β₂ : Bound Short-Range SP — Bound SP mode localized at the ε₃ (low-index) side. Exhibits tighter field confinement and higher propagation loss.

━━ βL₁ : Leaky Mode — Leaky mode with transverse field sign combination (+S₁, −S₃). Radiates energy into the semi-infinite ε₃ substrate.

━━ βL₂ : Leaky Mode — Leaky mode with transverse field sign combination (−S₁, +S₃). Radiates energy into the ε₁ superstrate.

Analytic Dispersion Relation
Single-interface SP(∞) exact solution:
β = (ω/c) · √[ (εd · εm) / (εd + εm) ]

Thin-film three-layer implicit equation (solved via Muller's Root Finding):
tanh(S₂·h) = -[S₂(S₁/ε₁ + S₃/ε₃)] / [S₂²/ε² + (S₁·S₃)/(ε₁·ε₃)]
where Si = √(β² - εi·(ω/c)²) represents the transverse wavenumber component.

Reference Lines

┄┄ SP₁(∞) — Analytical single-interface dispersion curve at the semi-infinite metal / ε₁ (top) boundary.

┄┄ SP₃(∞) — Analytical single-interface dispersion curve at the semi-infinite metal / ε₃ (bottom) boundary.

┄┄ Light lines — Photon momentum lines β = √(ε) · ω/c. Below these lines, the bound modes become radiative (cutoff point) as they intersect the bulk propagating regime.

Other Plots & Metrics

Attenuation (top-right): Visualizes the imaginary part Im(β). Higher values correspond to shorter propagation lengths due to Drude damping.

Field Profile (bottom-right): Transverse cross-section of intensity |f(z)|² spanning across the waveguide stack at the selected sampling frequency ω.

Propagation Length:
Lp = 1 / (2 · Im[βphys]) (meters)
Measures the distance an SP wave travels before its intensity drops by a factor of 1/e.

Implementation Details & Algorithms

Numerical Techniques

Muller's Method: The core root-finding algorithm used to solve the implicit dispersion relation for the complex propagation constant β. Unlike Newton-Raphson, Muller's method does not require analytical derivatives and naturally handles complex roots by fitting a parabola through three initial points at each step.

Parameter Continuation: To trace out the continuous dispersion curves over the frequency grid (k₀), the solver uses the converged solution β(k₀) as the starting initial guess for the next frequency step β(k₀ + Δk₀). This ensures numerical stability and prevents the solver from jumping between different polariton mode branches.

Single-Interface Asymptotics: At the very first frequency point (lowest k₀), the solver initializes Muller's method using the exact analytical solutions for the two single-interface surface plasmons (SP₁ and SP₃) evaluated at infinite film thickness (h → ∞).

Branch Selection & Leaky Modes

Square Root Branching: The transverse decay constant S = √(β² - εk₀²) requires careful branch selection on the complex plane. The principal square root is taken and mathematically forced to yield Re(S) > 0, which corresponds to exponentially decaying bound fields required by boundary conditions at infinity.

Leaky Wave Detection: Modes that cross the light line (photon momentum lines) cease to be perfectly bound. To find these leaky modes, the solver intentionally flips the sign of S in either the top or bottom cladding, effectively searching for waves that grow exponentially in the transverse direction, radiating power into the bulk dielectrics.

Wiki: Parameters & Acronyms

Core Acronyms

  • SP: Surface Polariton (or Surface Plasmon). An electromagnetic wave coupled to plasma oscillations at a metal-dielectric interface.
  • LRSP / SRSP: Long-Range / Short-Range Surface Polariton. In thin films, the two interfaces couple, creating a symmetric mode (Long-Range, lower loss) and an antisymmetric mode (Short-Range, higher loss).
  • TM: Transverse Magnetic. Surface polaritons only exist for TM (p-polarized) light where the magnetic field is strictly parallel to the interface.

Physical Parameters Glossary

  • ε₁ (Top) / ε₃ (Bottom): The real, positive relative permittivity of the semi-infinite dielectric cladding layers.
  • ε_m: The complex permittivity of the central metal film, evaluated via the Drude model. Must have a negative real part to support an SP.
  • ω (Omega): The absolute angular frequency of the excitation wave [rad/s].
  • ω_p (Plasma Freq): The natural oscillation frequency of the free electron gas in the metal. The resonance frequency asymptote.
  • Γ (Gamma): The electron collision rate in the metal, dictating ohmic losses (damping) and placing a floor on the mode's lifespan.
  • h: The physical geometric thickness of the metal film [nm].
  • k₀: Free-space wave-number (k₀ = ω/c). It represents the momentum of a photon in a vacuum.
  • β (Beta): The complex propagation constant of the SP wave. Re(β) governs phase velocity; Im(β) governs propagation loss in the guided direction.
  • S (Decay Constant): The transverse wave-number component. S = √(β² - εk₀²). Governs how far the fields penetrate away from the interface in the perpendicular z-axis.
  • L_p: Propagation Length. Distance over which the wave's power decays by 1/e due to absorptive or radiative losses.
